\begin{abstract}
Temporal graphs whose edges are immutable timestamped events---system
audit streams, agent execution traces, interaction logs---are ingested
as never-ending, near-sorted streams; queried along time and causality
(time-respecting reachability, point-in-time reconstruction, windowed
aggregation); and retained under time-based policies. No deployed
storage layer serves this combination: entity-keyed dynamic graph
stores demote the timestamp to a payload attribute, columnar and LSM
engines answer range filters well but pay a join per traversal hop,
and statically time-sorted layouts win queries while being unable to
ingest at all.

Barry keys storage on time itself. Each timestamp maps to a virtual
address by a constant-time shift, and the operating system's demand
paging absorbs temporal sparsity, so no index is built or maintained.
Every property the workload needs then falls out of the layout:
ingestion is a lock-free $O(1)$ append, independent of arrival order;
retention is a page release over an address prefix; compaction is
explicit and off the ingest path---queries stay correct without it,
and stay correct \emph{during} concurrent ingestion via a per-slot
release-publication protocol, soaked at a billion edges with zero
violations; durability follows from state being a pure function of the
edge multiset, so recovery is re-ingestion.

Barry sustains 3.3--6.8M~events/s of continuous ingestion (4.2M
durable, at the disk's sequential bandwidth) while answering
time-respecting traversal $150$--$700\times$ faster than a columnar
engine on identical data with element-wise identical answers. Against
a statically time-sorted CSR it concedes $2.7\times$ on traversal
latency---and never stops being queryable, where queries served
during the CSR's mandatory rebuild silently lose $33\%$ of the true
answer. At the storage layer, classic graph algorithms cost
$1.4$--$2.1\times$ the static topology optimum; time-windowed
traversal at provenance-scale degrees runs $104\times$ ahead of
arrival-order adjacency. The driving workload is provenance: on 1.05
billion real enterprise events with 749 labelled red-team actions and
on DARPA TC whole-system traces, Barry traces every real alert in
seconds total and reconstructs full attack chains at microsecond
latency---while our measurements show the lossy reduction today's
pipelines rely on silently destroys 44\% of attack evidence, the case
for the lossless streaming retention this engine makes affordable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{The workload.}
Every hardened endpoint ships an audit pipeline; every serious agent
framework logs its execution. Both produce the same object: a
\emph{provenance graph}, whose vertices are principals (processes,
files, sockets, hosts, tools, memory entries) and whose edges are
timestamped events between them. The graph is append-only---events
happen and are never updated---and it is interrogated along exactly
two axes: time and causality. The canonical queries are backward
tracing (\emph{which events could have influenced this
alert?}~\cite{king2003backtracker}), forward impact (\emph{what did
this compromised principal touch afterwards?}), point-in-time
reconstruction, and windowed aggregation.

\paragraph{The mismatch.}
No deployed storage layer serves these queries from a live stream.
High-performance dynamic graph stores---PMA-based
(PPCSR~\cite{wheatman2021ppcsr}, DCSR~\cite{shen2026dcsr}), tree-based
(Aspen~\cite{dhulipala2019aspen}), adjacency-list-based
(Sortledton~\cite{fuchs2023sortledton})---key storage on
source-vertex identity and demote the timestamp to a payload
attribute, so a time-respecting query degrades to a structural scan
with per-edge filtering. Column stores and LSM engines invert the
failure: ordered by time, they excel at range filters but pay a full
join against the edge table for every traversal hop
(\S\ref{sec:eval-traversal}). Versioned temporal graph databases
(AeonG~\cite{hou2024aeong}, TVA~\cite{li2026tva}) solve a different
problem entirely: efficient retrieval of \emph{updated entity
versions}, where the core difficulty is version-chain organization.
Provenance events have no versions---nothing is ever updated---and the
queries are reachability, not snapshot retrieval.

\paragraph{What the mismatch costs today.}
The practical consequence is that the field throws data away. We
measured a single server under an ordinary parallel build emitting
$80{,}021$ provenance-relevant system calls per second
(\S\ref{sec:workload}); the DARPA Transparent Computing corpora record
roughly $102$ events per second per host---meaning current pipelines
discard on the order of $783$ of every $784$ events before storage.
Reduction systems make this rigorous: causality-preserving reduction
(CPR) and successors~\cite{zhang2025tapas} report three orders of
magnitude of compression while preserving detection accuracy. What no
prior work measures is what reduction does to the \emph{evidence an
investigator must follow}. Section~\ref{sec:evidence} shows, on 1.05
billion real events with real red-team ground truth, that the answer
is: it destroys it, and the standard validation metric cannot see the
destruction.

\paragraph{Our design.}
Barry begins from an address-space observation: a 64-bit virtual
address space is four orders of magnitude larger than the time range
of any recorded workload. Rather than maintaining an index from
timestamp to location, Barry \emph{makes the timestamp the location}:
\begin{equation}
A(T) \;=\; A_{\mathrm{base}} + \big((T - T_{\mathrm{start}}) \gg S\big)\times P,
\label{eq:mapping}
\end{equation}
a subtraction and a shift, with the operating system's demand paging
absorbing temporal sparsity at zero data-structure cost
(Theorems~\ref{thm:addr},~\ref{thm:space}). Every design property the
provenance workload needs then falls out of the layout rather than
being engineered on top: $O(1)$ lock-free append
(Theorem~\ref{thm:append}) because an edge's home is computed, not
searched; order-independence because out-of-order arrival lands at the
same address; retention as a page release over an address prefix; and
time-respecting traversal that touches only the windows a query names.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{A streaming temporal graph storage engine}
  (\S\ref{sec:design}) whose primary key is time: constant-time
  addressing with a physical-space bound under demand paging, adaptive
  granularity selection, lock-free order-independent ingestion, a
  release-publication protocol making queries correct during
  concurrent ingestion, an explicit-compaction contract that keeps the
  ingest path $O(1)$, address-prefix retention, and durability by
  recovery-as-re-ingestion---each backed by a theorem in the artifact.
\item \textbf{A layered evaluation} (\S\ref{sec:eval}): element-wise
  answer-verified comparisons against DuckDB and three CSR layouts, a
  rebuild-versus-stream experiment that measures what static
  structures silently lose, a storage-read decomposition isolating the
  engine's share of classic graph algorithms, and a billion-edge
  concurrency soak---on 14 datasets and 7 baselines.
\item \textbf{A workload characterization with measured stakes}
  (\S\ref{sec:workload}): per-host event rates across workload
  classes, the reduction ratio current pipelines silently apply, and
  ground-truth measurements---attack-edge survival under reduction,
  full-chain recovery on DARPA TC---establishing that lossless
  streaming retention is both necessary and, with this engine,
  affordable at interactive latency.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Driving Workload: Temporal Event Streams}
\label{sec:workload}

An event-stream temporal graph is any graph whose edges are immutable
timestamped occurrences: audit events, agent tool calls, messages,
transactions, telemetry. We characterize the most demanding instance
we know of---whole-system provenance---because it stresses every axis
at once: rate, retention, query class, and the cost of getting
retention wrong. The engine is not specific to it.

\subsection{Event rates, measured}
We instrumented an ordinary 20-core server (syscall counts via strace,
rates against separately measured untraced wall-clock) across four
workload classes and observed per-host provenance-relevant syscall
rates---opens, reads, writes, execs, clones, connects: precisely the
event classes whole-system provenance
records~\cite{pasquier2017camflow}---of \textbf{8{,}300/s under
archival I/O, 10{,}700/s under file analytics, and 58{,}500--80{,}000/s
under a parallel software build} ($\sim$20/s idle). For calibration:
the DARPA TC THEIA trace stores $102.2$ events/s/host; the LANL
enterprise corpus (authentication only) stores $0.012$. Recorded
corpora therefore sit \emph{two to three orders of magnitude} below
what active hosts emit---$783\times$ for THEIA against the build-rate
peak---and that gap is reduction, applied before storage, whose cost
has never been measured on the investigation axis. The measurement
script ships in the artifact.

\subsection{The retention arithmetic}
At 20 bytes per event, losslessly retaining a 1{,}000-host fleet at
the measured rate is 138\,TB/day; a 100\,TB budget holds 17 hours.
Lossless retention of raw whole-fleet provenance is not a realistic
posture, and Barry does not claim to make it one. The design question
is different: \emph{given that a reduction point must be chosen, can
it be chosen with its investigative cost visible, and can the storage
layout push that point outward?} Sections~\ref{sec:evidence}
and~\ref{sec:eval} answer the two halves.

\subsection{Why the query axis is time}
Three structural properties separate provenance from the workloads
existing graph stores were built for. (1)~\emph{Append-only}: events
are immutable; there are no versions, no in-place updates, and
deletion is retention policy, not semantics. (2)~\emph{Monotone
predicates}: every canonical query carries a time predicate that is
monotone along any path it explores---taint propagates only forward in
time, causality only backward~\cite{king2003backtracker}. A layout
whose primary axis is time turns these predicates into address
arithmetic. (3)~\emph{Continuous small-batch arrival}: audit streams
arrive perpetually and near-sorted but not exactly sorted; spans stay
open for hours and events straggle. Structures that depend on global
order (sorted arrays, PMAs, LSM levels) pay for arrival order; a
computed address does not.

\subsection{The stakes, measured on ground truth}
\label{sec:evidence}

We use the LANL Comprehensive Multi-Source Cyber-Security Events
corpus~\cite{lanl2015data}: 58 consecutive days of a real enterprise
network, $1{,}051{,}430{,}459$ authentication events over $17{,}666$
hosts, with \textbf{749 labelled red-team compromise events} as ground
truth---to our knowledge the largest public corpus pairing raw events
with attacker labels. All 749 resolve into the graph's identifier
space; 706 match a stored authentication edge exactly.

\subsubsection{Attack-edge survival vs.\ node-level recall}
\label{sec:evidence-survival}
We apply causality-preserving reduction at operating windows from 60
seconds to 24 hours and ask two questions of the reduced log.
\emph{Question 1 (the field's metric):} is the compromised host set
still reachable from the intrusion point? \emph{Question 2 (the
investigator's):} do the red team's own edges still exist?

\subsubsection{No usable investigation horizon}
\label{sec:evidence-window}
Forward taint from the first red-team compromise, sweeping the

\subsubsection{Cross-corpus, briefly}
\label{sec:evidence-cross}
The protocol repeats on DARPA TC E3 CADETS (22.5M information-flow
edges over 2.86M system-level entities, four report-documented
attacks resolved to node ids). Granularity flips every constant:
bidirectional tracing recovers 68--100\% of each attack chain within
impact sets of 0.3--1.6\% of the graph (against 15--29\% of the
network at LANL's host granularity); CPR's achievable reduction drops
from $131\times$ to a $5.6\times$ ceiling---coarse logs' apparent
redundancy \emph{is} causal detail---and at that ceiling it erases a
third of the chain of precisely the attack that reused C2
infrastructure from earlier intrusions. The engine-relevant constant
is that every one of these measurements is a time-respecting
traversal answered in microseconds to milliseconds
(\S\ref{sec:eval}).

\section{Design}
\label{sec:design}

Barry's architecture has four components: the \emph{Timeline}, an
mmap'd array of window slots indexed by
Equation~\eqref{eq:mapping}; the \emph{Arena}, a bump-allocated
region of cache-line-aligned edge blocks chained per window; the
\emph{Vertex Index}, per-vertex bucket chains of 16-byte edge
references (with an opt-in reverse direction for backward tracing);
and the \emph{retention layer}, which releases physical pages over a
time prefix. Full proofs of the five theorems below ship in the
artifact; we state them and the design decisions they pin down.

\subsection{Addressing and space}
\begin{theorem}[Constant-time temporal addressing]
\label{thm:addr}
For any timestamp $t$, the timeline slot holding every edge stamped
$t$ is computed in $O(1)$ worst case with no data-dependent memory
access.
\end{theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Physical-space bound]
\label{thm:space}
With page size $P$ and $W_{occ}$ non-empty windows, resident timeline
memory is at most $W_{occ}\cdot P$ plus lazily-materialized page-table
overhead; untouched windows cost zero bytes.
\end{theorem}
This is the ``zero-index'' claim in its defensible form: sparsity is
absorbed by the MMU, not by a structure Barry maintains. The mapping
is \emph{anonymous} memory---no file backing---so the well-known
failure modes of file-backed mmap buffer
management~\cite{crotty2022mmap} (torn pages, SIGBUS on truncation,
eviction stalls) do not arise; Barry uses mmap the way an allocator
reserves address space, not as a buffer pool.

\subsection{Adaptive granularity}
The shift $S$ sets the window width. Too coarse and windows over-fill;
too fine and the timeline sprawls. Barry selects $S$ from aggregate
stats at load, or incrementally from a stream prefix:
\begin{theorem}[Granularity bound]
\label{thm:shift}
For $E \ge 2\cdot\mathrm{target}$, the selected shift yields mean
window occupancy $\mu \in [\mathrm{target}/2,\, 4\cdot\mathrm{target})$.
\end{theorem}
The constant 4 is honest: one factor of two from rounding the window
to a power of two (shift addressing admits nothing finer), one from
the ceiling on the window count. Datasets in our corpus span
per-window densities from 32 to 32{,}768 edges under a single target;
the bound holds at both extremes.

\subsection{Lock-free ingestion, explicit compaction}
\begin{theorem}[Bounded-step append]
\label{thm:append}
Absent arena exhaustion, an insert completes in $O(1+c)$ steps where
$c$ counts CAS conflicts, and some insert completes at every step
(lock-freedom). Slot claims and arena bumps are individually
wait-free.
\end{theorem}
Appends never sort. Consequently a vertex's bucket holds refs in
arrival order, and time-respecting expansion is $O(d)$ per vertex. An
\emph{explicit} compaction pass (\texttt{finalize()}) sorts each
bucket by timestamp once, after which expansion binary-searches in
$O(\log d + k)$. Compaction is deliberately not on the insert path:
folding it in would make every batch pay $O(E)$ and forfeit streaming
(we measured exactly this failure: an early build that compacted per
batch ingested at $11$K events/s; moved off the path, $3.28$M).
Queries are correct in either state---compaction changes cost, not
answers---and they are correct \emph{during} concurrent ingestion:
writers publish each edge by storing its timestamp last with release
semantics (zeroed pages make an unpublished slot detectable), readers
acquire-load and skip unpublished slots, and the sorted-bucket flag is
cleared before the count is bumped so that observing the flag proves
the loaded count covers only the verified prefix. A multi-writer,
multi-reader stress test observes zero violations, and a
billion-edge soak---12 writers ingesting all of LANL at
6.8M~events/s while 4 readers validated 780M delivered edges across
48{,}636 queries---observes zero violations with the final count
exact. Barry's contract is thus: \emph{always
appendable, always correct---including mid-ingest; fast expansion
after a compaction that can be scheduled whenever convenient.}

\subsection{Taint semantics}
\begin{theorem}[Sparse taint correctness]
\label{thm:taint}
\texttt{forward\_taint}$(v_0,t_0,t_{hi})$ returns exactly the
vertices reachable by time-respecting paths with their earliest
contamination times; backward tracing is its mirror under time
reversal.
\end{theorem}
The traversal is a temporal Dijkstra over a sparse visited map: cost
scales with the answer, not with $|V|$. Replacing an earlier dense
formulation ($O(V)$ per query) with this sparse one is worth
$11{,}011\times$ on a 3.8M-vertex graph and is verified
vertex-for-vertex against the dense fixpoint in the test suite.

\subsection{Retention}
Retention is a monotone time predicate, so under
Equation~\eqref{eq:mapping} it is an address-prefix operation:
\texttt{evict\_before}$(t)$ walks the timeline prefix, merges block
extents, and releases whole pages with \texttt{MADV\_DONTNEED}---no
scan of the edge set, no tombstone compaction, readers of other
windows undisturbed. On a 16.5M-edge graph, dropping 90\% of the
history takes 18\,ms and returns 331\,MB.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:impl}
Barry is $\sim$6{,}300 lines of C++17 with no mandatory dependencies:
engine, nine graph algorithms, the provenance traversals, retention,
and converters for the LANL format and DARPA CDM. Range scans over
block interiors are vectorized (AVX2 gathers over the packed 20-byte
edges); interior windows of a range query are copied without any
timestamp comparison since only boundary windows can contain
out-of-range edges. The test suite includes an end-to-end synthetic
attack chain that validates converter, direction policy, and both
traversals against hand-computed ground truth.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\textbf{Setup.} 20-core Xeon, 188\,GB RAM. Datasets: LANL (1.05B
events, ground truth), DARPA TC E3 CADETS (44.4M CDM records $\to$
22.5M information-flow edges over 2.86M system-level entities, with
the four nation-state attacks of the official ground-truth report
resolved to node ids), 12 SNAP temporal graphs (60K--646M edges), the
synthetic chain fixture. Baselines: DuckDB v1.5.5 via its C API in
the same process; a time-sorted CSR (\texttt{tcsr}: entity-keyed,
per-vertex time-sorted---the strongest simple competitor); an
arrival-order entity CSR (\texttt{ucsr}: what an ingesting
entity-first store holds between rebuilds); the topology-only CSR;
Neo4j 4.4; DCSR/PPCSR harnesses. \emph{Every cross-engine comparison
in this section verifies element-wise identical answers before any
latency is reported.} Latency on this workload is heavy-tailed
(median-to-p95 spans five orders of magnitude); we report
distributions or size-binned medians, never means.

\subsection{Traversal and filter vs.\ a columnar engine}
\label{sec:eval-traversal}
Multi-hop time-respecting traversal to fixpoint, binned by answer
size: Barry leads DuckDB by $150$--$700\times$ at every bin;
at $|R|>1000$ the median is 12.0\,ms vs.\ 2.43\,s ($203\times$). The
gap is a data-model consequence---each hop is a fresh join against
the edge table---and grows with the answer. On DuckDB's home
ground, time-range filtering, Barry's vectorized scan wins the median
$10.6\times$ (77\,$\mu$s vs.\ 819\,$\mu$s across five seeds) with
the p95 at parity (981\,$\mu$s vs.\ 1{,}105\,$\mu$s, overlapping
ranges). An earlier build lost the tail $4\times$ to DuckDB's
sequential scan; a header-only reserve prepass, an interior-window
bulk path, and a block-publication watermark closed it, and we report
the progression so the traversal numbers can be believed.

\subsection{Rebuild versus stream}
\label{sec:eval-staleness}
The time-sorted CSR answers a single traversal $2.7\times$ faster
than Barry at high degree. Its only update path is a full rebuild:
28.4\,s on LANL. Queries served from the pre-rebuild snapshot are not
stale but \emph{wrong}: over the live window they silently lose
33.2\% of the true answer on average and 99.5\% in the worst case
(68/100 queries incomplete; the independent wiki-talk measurement
gives 34.1\%). Barry ingests the same arrivals incrementally at
3.28\,M~events/s and never has a blind window. Barry's own optional
compaction costs 76.3\,s on LANL---more than the CSR rebuild, and we
report it---but sits on the performance path, not the correctness
path: uncompacted Barry answers correctly at $O(d)$ expansion.

\subsection{Sustained triage on real ground truth}
All 749 red-team alerts, backward-traced to fixpoint over a 14-hour
window each: \textbf{46.5\,s total} (median 24.6\,$\mu$s, p95
353\,ms). At an enterprise volume of $10^4$ alerts/day this is ten
minutes of one core---tracing \emph{everything} stops being a budget
decision, where the columnar path (2.43\,s per heavy trace) makes it
a 6.7-hour one. The same sweep quantifies dependency explosion (2,313
ancestors per alert on average), reinforcing
\S\ref{sec:evidence-window}: speed is necessary for triage, and
demonstrably not sufficient for containment.

\subsection{The storage-read share of graph algorithms}
\label{sec:eval-e13}
End-to-end algorithm times conflate the storage engine with the
algorithm implementation. E13 separates them: four engines deliver
each vertex's adjacency into an identical scratch buffer under a
timer; the algorithm consumes it outside the timer; checksums verify
all four engines drive identical executions. On structure-only
algorithms (BFS, PageRank, label propagation) Barry's storage-read
time is within $1.7$--$2.1\times$ of the static topology-only
CSR---the structural optimum---while being the only engine of the
four that ingests; at LANL scale (685M deliveries) the concession
shrinks to $1.4\times$. On time-windowed BFS the ordering flips with
degree: at mean degree 20 the arrival-order entity store wins on
contiguity, at 337 Barry reaches parity, and at LANL's mean degree of
59{,}517 Barry leads it by $104\times$ (4.2\,ms vs.\ 435\,ms)---the
crossover lands exactly where endpoint provenance lives, because
sorted-bucket pruning touches only the queried window while an
arrival-order adjacency must scan every entry. The time-sorted CSR
leads throughout (0.5\,ms) and cannot ingest, which
\S\ref{sec:eval-staleness} prices. Structure-insensitivity is a
bounded, measured cost at the storage layer---not the $5$--$15\times$
that end-to-end comparisons against tuned algorithm suites
suggest---and it buys the temporal properties everything else in this
paper measures.

\subsection{Scale and footprint}
LANL loads in 255\,s at 96\,GB RSS (1.05B edges, reverse index on);
the 646M-edge reddit corpus runs in 39.6\,GB. Peak small-batch
ingestion reaches 13--16M events/s on small graphs and 3.28M
sustained on LANL's degree-59{,}517 topology. The vertex index is
roughly half of resident memory; the reverse direction, needed only
for backward tracing, adds 39--58\% RSS and is opt-in.

\subsection{Negative results, kept}
For the record: (i)~caching timestamps in edge refs (+50\% index
memory for 15\% traversal) was built, measured, and reverted;
(ii)~binary-search expansion is worth $2.5\times$ at degree 337 and
nothing below degree 30; (iii)~DuckDB's \texttt{DELETE} reclaims
memory as well as Barry's eviction does---the retention win is speed
($5$--$29\times$), not reclamation, and a nightly retention pass does
not need it; (iv)~Barry's compaction is currently slower than a CSR
rebuild (arena pointer-chase per ref; an arena-order resolve pass
would fix it and is future work).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Versioned temporal graph stores.} TVA~\cite{li2026tva} and
AeonG~\cite{hou2024aeong} organize \emph{versions of updated entities}
for snapshot retrieval; their central difficulties (delta encoding,
version-chain locality, hopscotch layouts) do not arise in an
append-only event stream, and their query class (snapshot + predicate)
is disjoint from time-respecting reachability. The two lines are
complementary: an entity's property history is a TVA problem; what the
entity causally touched is Barry's.
\textbf{Structural dynamic graph stores}~\cite{wheatman2021ppcsr,
shen2026dcsr, dhulipala2019aspen, fuchs2023sortledton} optimize the
entity axis and treat time as payload; \S\ref{sec:eval-e13} bounds
what Barry concedes on their axis, and no member of the family
answers P1/P2 natively.
\textbf{Columnar/LSM engines}~\cite{raasveldt2019duckdb} own the
range-filter tail (\S\ref{sec:eval-traversal}) and lose the traversal
class by two to three orders of magnitude.
\textbf{Provenance reduction}~\cite{zhang2025tapas, hassan2019nodoze}
validates against detection metrics; \S\ref{sec:evidence} supplies
the missing metric and the first ground-truth measurement of its
divergence.
\textbf{Agent memory systems}~\cite{rasmussen2025zep} have
independently converged on bi-temporal knowledge graphs for agent
state, confirming the workload's second incarnation; their storage
layer is an entity-keyed database, i.e.\ the mismatch of
\S\ref{sec:workload} transplanted.

\section{When Not to Use Barry}
\label{sec:limits}
Queries with no monotone time predicate get no help from the layout
and pay its overheads: whole-graph analytics over all time belong on
a static CSR built offline. Range filters at massive selectivity are
served equally well by a column store, which also holds the better
tail. Payloads beyond the 20-byte skeleton belong in an object store
by reference. Durability exploits the same property that simplifies
everything else: engine state is a pure function of the edge
multiset, so recovery \emph{is} re-ingestion. The WAL frames raw
batches with a CRC (torn tails detected and discarded, verified by a
crash-simulation test); an asynchronous writer overlaps log I/O with
insertion, and durable ingestion sustains 4.2M~events/s under 64\,MB
group commit against 7.4M volatile---at which point the WAL is bound
by the disk's sequential bandwidth (156\,MB/s here), not by the
engine (1.2M with per-batch fsync for the strictest durability);
\texttt{flush\_before}$(t)$ tiers a cold time prefix to a loadable
segment file and releases its pages in one pass, fsync'd before
eviction. WAL appends are not yet overlapped with inserts, and
queries over flushed segments require reloading them; both are
engineering, not design, gaps.

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
Keying graph storage on time collapses the machinery a temporal event
stream otherwise requires: the index becomes a shift, ingestion a
lock-free append indifferent to arrival order, retention a page
release, durability a replay, and time-respecting traversal an
address computation---correct even mid-ingest, at a measured
$150$--$700\times$ over columnar layouts and $104\times$ over
arrival-order adjacency at realistic degrees. The concessions are
bounded and priced: $2.7\times$ traversal latency against a static
structure that cannot ingest and silently loses a third of its
answers while rebuilding, and $1.4$--$2.1\times$ at the storage layer
on structure-only analytics. Provenance supplied the stakes---on
real ground truth, the retention this engine makes affordable is the
difference between evidence that survives and evidence silently
destroyed---and the same contract serves every workload whose edges
are events: agent traces, telemetry, interaction streams. The
security semantics that these measurements open---recovery under
known contamination, reduction policies scored by evidence
survival---are the subject of work this engine now makes possible.
